## Technical Specifications & Protocol Amendments

The following protocol modules establish the technical mechanisms governing decentralized identity, boundary isolation, and general-purpose cross-platform orchestration:

### 4.1 Identity Engine
Maintains persistent identity continuity across digital environments.

*   **Sub-Protocol 4.1.1: Institutional Precedent & Cryptographic Standards**  
    Platform adoption is driven through regulatory liability structures mimicking global Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) mandates. The Identity Engine natively interfaces with W3C Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) Core v1.0 and Verifiable Credentials Data Model v2.0 standards. To achieve machine-speed verification without tracking rails, the framework operationalizes BBS+ Cryptographic Signatures for zero-knowledge selective disclosure.
*   **Sub-Protocol 4.1.2: Architectural Arbitrations & Constrained Disclosure**  
    To resolve the tension between identity continuity and unlinkability, membranes evaluate incoming tokens via *Cryptographic Accumulator-Based Revocation Status Lists*, requiring a Zero-Knowledge Proof of Non-Membership. Identity de-anonymization (The Redress Gate) rejects backdoors and is governed strictly via *Threshold Cryptography Protocols (Shamir’s Secret Sharing)* fractionalized across independent vectors.

### 4.6 Cross-System Coordination Engine
Facilitates information exchange and coordinated response across multiple digital environments.

*   **Sub-Protocol 4.6.1: Decentralized Signal Normalization & General-Purpose Orchestration**  
    Introduces a unified, general-purpose cross-platform orchestration layer consolidating fragmented silos (e.g., PhotoDNA, GIFCT, StopNCII). Platforms are structurally barred from broadcasting raw data logs. Local Harmonics Engines compress flagged behavioral topologies into a standardized *Systemic Risk Vector (SRV)* mapping to the document’s explicit Harm Taxonomy, enabling deterministic ledger consensus and cross-vector threat intelligence.
